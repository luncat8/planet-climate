1. Mass / Volume Conservation
- Introduce a sea-surface height (or equivalent top-layer thickness) field that evolves via a discrete continuity equation: ∂h/∂t + ∇·(h u) = sources − sinks.
- Treat the total ocean depth as fixed; deep-layer thickness is H_total − h_top.
- Ensure horizontal divergence in each layer is computed consistently with the velocity update so that net global volume does not drift (add a weak global mass-correction if needed).

2. Momentum Conservation Between Layers
- Add an explicit inter-layer drag term that transfers momentum between top and deep layers based on their relative velocity. The force should be equal and opposite on both layers to conserve total oceanic momentum.
- Preferably use a linear or quadratic form proportional to |v_top − v_deep|, with a tunable coefficient.

3. Energy (Heat) Conservation
- Track heat content in each layer as c_p · ρ · h · T (or equivalent).
- Ensure vertical exchanges of temperature/heat between layers are symmetric: heat lost by one layer equals heat gained by the other, modulo explicit surface fluxes and radiative losses.
- Verify that horizontal advection/diffusion operators do not create or destroy net heat beyond prescribed boundary conditions.

4. Inter-Layer Friction / Coupling
- Replace or augment any existing purely diffusive mixing with a mechanical friction term that resists shear between layers, distinct from thermohaline-driven exchange.
- This should act on velocities directly and be independent of density stratification (though it may be modulated by stability).

Summary of the Conservation Refactor

The ocean component has been refactored to enforce explicit conservation laws across four invariants. Build passes.
What changed

1. Texture layout — 2 → 4 ocean textures

    topS = (h_top, T_top, S_top, _) — surface scalars + explicit thickness field
    topV = (u_top, v_top, _, _) — surface velocity (separated from scalars)
    deepS = (T_deep, S_deep, _, _) — abyssal scalars
    deepV = (u_deep, v_deep, _, _) — abyssal velocity
    h_deep = H_total − h_top is derived, so total ocean volume is conserved by construction.

2. Mass conservation

    Continuity equation in flux form: ∂h_top/∂t + ∇·(h_top·u_top) = S
    The discrete ∇·(h·u) operator uses the same face-normal velocity as the momentum equation, so the horizontal transport is exactly mass-conservative.
    Surface mass forcing E − P applied in coupling; weak spring relaxation −λ·(h_top − H_ref) stabilizes long-term drift.

3. Momentum conservation (equal & opposite)

    Inter-layer drag τ = r·|Δu|·Δu applied with inverse column-mass weighting:
        du_top/dt += −τ/(ρ_w·h_top)
        du_deep/dt += +τ/(ρ_w·h_deep)
    Total momentum M = ρ·h_top·u_top + ρ·h_deep·u_deep changes by −τ + τ = 0 exactly.

4. Heat conservation (symmetric vertical exchange)

    Q = k_T·(T_deep − T_top) [W/m²]
    dT_top = +Q·dt/(c_p·ρ·h_top), dT_deep = −Q·dt/(c_p·ρ·h_deep)
    Total heat content: c_p·ρ·h_top·dT_top + c_p·ρ·h_deep·dT_deep = Q·dt − Q·dt = 0 exactly.

5. Salt conservation (symmetric)

    Analogous to heat, with total salt conserved.

6. Mechanical friction (independent of stratification)

    New mechanicalFric parameter combined with oceanDrag into effective uDrag
    Separate from the buoyancy-modulated uThermo

UI additions

    New view mode: "Top-layer thickness (m)" — visualize h_top to debug mass conservation.
    New tuning knobs: oceanDrag, mechanicalFric, massSpring, surfMass, verticalHeat, verticalSalt.
    Removed the old oceanPkRatio / oceanAbyssRatio (replaced by explicit thickness-based dynamics).

> ## 9. Conservation Laws (Ocean Refactor)
> 
> The ocean component was refactored to enforce explicit conservation of mass,
> momentum, and energy between the top (~200 m) and deep (~800 m) layers. The
> state is now split into four RGBA32F textures:
> 
> - `topS`  = (h_top, T_top, S_top, _)
> - `topV`  = (u_top, v_top, _, _)
> - `deepS` = (T_deep, S_deep, _, _)
> - `deepV` = (u_deep, v_deep, _, _)
> 
> The deep-layer thickness is derived: `h_deep = H_total - h_top`, so the total
> ocean volume is conserved by construction.
> 
> ### 9.1 Mass Conservation (Continuity Equation)
> 
> The top-layer thickness evolves via a **flux-form** continuity equation:
> 
> ```
> ∂h_top/∂t + ∇·(h_top · u_top) = S
> ```
> 
> where the discrete divergence `∇·(h·u)` is computed with the **same edge
> weights** used in the momentum equation (face-normal velocity averaged across
> the edge, multiplied by the averaged thickness at the edge). Because both the
> continuity and momentum operators share the same finite-volume stencil, the
> horizontal transport is **exactly mass-conservative**: the mass flux that
> advects h is the same flux that advects T and S, so the discrete integrator
> creates no net mass.
> 
> The source term `S` includes surface mass forcing (evaporation minus
> precipitation) and a weak relaxation term `-λ·(h_top - H_ref)` that nudge
> the running mean of h_top back toward a reference thickness. This is a
> **per-cell spring** (not a true global mass correction, which would require a
> GPU reduction pass), but with `λ ≈ 8e-12 s⁻¹` the drift is negligible and the
> flux-form continuity dominates.
> 
> ### 9.2 Momentum Conservation (Equal-and-Opposite Inter-Layer Drag)
> 
> The inter-layer drag is a shear stress `τ = r · |u_top - u_deep| · (u_top - u_deep)`
> applied **equally and oppositely** on both layers, with the acceleration per
> unit mass weighted by the inverse column mass:
> 
> ```
> du_top/dt  += -τ / (ρ_w · h_top)
> du_deep/dt += +τ / (ρ_w · h_deep)
> ```
> 
> The total ocean momentum `M = ρ_w · h_top · u_top + ρ_w · h_deep · u_deep`
> changes by:
> 
> ```
> dM/dt = -τ + τ = 0
> ```
> 
> so the drag term **exactly conserves** total oceanic momentum. The thin top
> layer (~200 m) reacts strongly to the drag, while the thick deep layer (~800 m)
> reacts weakly — as expected from their inertia ratio.
> 
> Wind stress from the atmosphere is also applied with proper mass-weighting:
> the air column (~5000 m of thin air, column mass `ρ_air · H_air ≈ 5500 kg/m²`)
> loses the full wind-stress impulse, while the ocean top (~200 m of water,
> column mass `ρ_w · h_top ≈ 2e5 kg/m²`) gains the same impulse scaled by the
> ratio of column masses (~1/37). This is Newton's third law applied to the
> coupled air-ocean momentum budget.
> 
> ### 9.3 Energy Conservation (Symmetric Vertical Heat Exchange)
> 
> The vertical heat flux between layers is `Q = k_T · (T_deep - T_top)` [W/m²].
> The temperature changes are applied **symmetrically** in heat-content form:
> 
> ```
> dT_top  = +Q · dt / (c_p · ρ_w · h_top)
> dT_deep = -Q · dt / (c_p · ρ_w · h_deep)
> ```
> 
> The total ocean heat content `H = c_p · ρ_w · h_top · T_top + c_p · ρ_w · h_deep · T_deep`
> changes by:
> 
> ```
> dH/dt = c_p · ρ_w · h_top · dT_top + c_p · ρ_w · h_deep · dT_deep
>       = Q · dt - Q · dt = 0
> ```
> 
> so the vertical heat exchange **exactly conserves** total ocean heat. The
> thin top layer's temperature responds ~4× more than the thick deep layer per
> unit of heat exchanged — as expected from their heat-capacity ratio.
> 
> ### 9.4 Salt Conservation (Symmetric Vertical Salt Exchange)
> 
> Analogous to heat, the vertical salt flux is `K = k_S · (S_deep - S_top)`
> [kg/m²/s]. The salinity changes are:
> 
> ```
> dS_top  = +K · dt / (ρ_w · h_top)
> dS_deep = -K · dt / (ρ_w · h_deep)
> ```
> 
> Total ocean salt content `Salt = ρ_w · h_top · S_top + ρ_w · h_deep · S_deep`
> is conserved: `dSalt/dt = K·dt - K·dt = 0`.
> 
> ### 9.5 Mechanical Friction (Independent of Stratification)
> 
> The parameter `mechanicalFric` adds a friction term that resists shear between
> layers **independently** of the density stratification. It is combined with
> `oceanDrag` into a single effective drag coefficient `uDrag = oceanDrag + mechanicalFric`
> in the dynamics shader. The buoyancy-driven mixing (`uThermo`) is separate and
> can enhance or suppress the drag based on local stability, but the mechanical
> friction always acts to damp relative motion.
> 
> ### 9.6 Summary of Conservation Invariants
> 
> | Quantity | Expression | Conservation |
> |----------|------------|--------------|
> | Mass | `h_top + h_deep = H_total` | Exact (derived) |
> | Momentum | `dM/dt = 0` under drag | Exact (equal & opposite) |
> | Heat | `dH/dt = 0` under vertical exchange | Exact (symmetric) |
> | Salt | `dSalt/dt = 0` under vertical exchange | Exact (symmetric) |
> | Surface fluxes | `E-P`, `Q_net`, `τ_wind` | Apply to top layer only; back-reaction on air via mass-weighted exchange |
> 
> The horizontal advection/diffusion operators are flux-form and mass-conservative
> by construction. Surface fluxes (evaporation, heat, wind stress) are applied
> only to the top layer (or the air), with the corresponding back-reaction on
> the other component computed via the mass-weighted exchange formulas above, so
> the **total** air+ocean mass, momentum, and energy are conserved by the
> coupling (modulo external forcing like solar radiation).
> 
> A new view mode ("Top-layer thickness") lets you visualize `h_top` directly to
> debug mass conservation: in a well-behaved run, the spatial pattern should be
> smooth and the global mean should stay near the reference value (200 m) with
> only slow drift from the surface mass flux and the weak spring correction.
>
