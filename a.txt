> ## 8. Post-mortem: fixing the tidally-locked ocean-inversion bug
> 
> A tidally-locked test (`omegaOrbit = 0`, sun fixed) exposed a real structural
> bug: the deep ocean raced from the hot side to the cold side while the
> surface layer sluggishly limped the other way — an inverted, deep-dominated
> circulation instead of the expected warm-surface-out / cold-deep-return
> overturning cell. Root causes and fixes:
> 
> 1. **Both ocean layers diagnosed pressure from their own local buoyancy with
>    the same sign**, and the deep layer's weight (`uPkDeep`) was set 4×
>    *larger* than the top layer's. That makes *both* layers independently flow
>    warm→cold, and the (over-)heavier deep layer dominates and drowns out the
>    real surface response — never mind reproducing an actual return limb.
>    **Fix:** the deep layer's pressure is now driven by the *negative* of the
>    TOP layer's buoyancy (`oceanPDeep = -k·buoy(top) + k_abyss·buoy(deep)`),
>    matching the real hydrostatic argument: a warm/light column above means
>    *less* weight overhead, hence *lower* pressure at depth beneath it — this
>    is precisely what turns the deep layer into a genuine, opposite-signed
>    return current instead of a scaled-up copy of the surface flow. The
>    compensation strength (`oceanPkRatio`, default 0.55× top) and a small
>    direct abyssal term (`oceanAbyssRatio`, default 0.12×) are both exposed as
>    tuning knobs.
> 2. **Vertical mixing was asymmetric** (`900·max(0, ρ_top-ρ_deep)`), so it only
>    accelerated sinking at the poles and never helped warm water upwell.
>    Changed to `900·abs(Δρ)` so either sign of instability stirs the column.
> 3. **Vertical momentum exchange between the ocean layers had the mass
>    weighting backwards**: the (thick, high-inertia) deep layer absorbed the
>    *full* top/deep velocity difference every mixing event while the (thin,
>    low-inertia) top layer lost almost none of it — literally the opposite of
>    what a 200 m vs 800 m layer-thickness ratio implies. It's now a
>    mass-conserving exchange weighted `0.8` (top) / `0.2` (deep), matching the
>    same 200 m/800 m assumption already used for the pressure terms and for
>    heat/salt exchange.
> 4. **Air–sea wind stress violated momentum conservation**: the ocean got the
>    *full* impulse and the air lost only 2% of it, pumping spurious kinetic
>    energy into the ocean and letting wind-driven currents overpower the
>    thermohaline signal. Fixed to a proper mass-weighted Newton's-3rd-law
>    exchange: `massRatio = (ρ_air·H_air)/(ρ_water·H_top) ≈ 1/37`, so
>    `airVel -= dstress; topVel += dstress·massRatio` — the ocean now barely
>    notices a gust that fully decelerates the wind, as it should.
> 5. **Rotation was a single overloaded parameter.** `omega` fed the Coriolis
>    term directly, so "locking" the planet for the tidal experiment (`omega=0`)
>    also silently killed Coriolis — but a real tidally-locked planet still
>    rotates once per orbit in inertial space and keeps a (weak) Coriolis
>    force. Split into:
>    * `omegaSpin` — physical rotation rate, feeds Coriolis only.
>    * `omegaOrbit` — angular rate of the sub-solar point (day/night cycle),
>      used only in `sunDir()`.
>    A "Tidally locked (freeze sun)" checkbox sets `omegaOrbit = 0` while
>    leaving `omegaSpin` (and therefore Coriolis) untouched.
> 
> One item from an earlier LLM-authored refactor pass was deliberately **not**
> taken: switching the globe's per-*vertex* field sampling to a per-*fragment*
> nearest-cell lookup (mirroring the equirectangular particle lookup texture).
> That would replace the smooth barycentric blend the rasteriser already gives
> across each triangle with a hard-edged Voronoi mosaic — i.e. it makes the
> hex-cell structure *more* visible, not less. Instead, faceting is reduced by
> (a) defaulting to subdivision **L6** (~41k cells) and (b) blending each
> vertex's value with a light-weight average of its 1-ring neighbours before
> rasterisation — cheap (a handful of extra `texelFetch` per vertex, done once,
> not per pixel), resolution-independent, and it actually softens the cell
> boundaries instead of hardening them.
