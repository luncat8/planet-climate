**Why they sat still**

Every coupling step **overwrote** air pressure from temperature (`P = P(T)`). Wind was then slaved to a standing thermal field (continents + a slow ocean), so cells could form but never leave. Three other things made that worse:

- Crank–Nicolson air Coriolis used an **f-plane** `f = 2Ω` (no β, no Rossby drift; same rotation at the equator as at the pole). The ocean already used `f = 2Ω sinφ`.
- Surface drag `1.6e-5 s⁻¹` killed a free vortex in **~17 hours**.
- `dP/dT = 60 Pa/K` only produced a **~2 m/s** thermal wind, too weak for eddies to beat friction.

Changing `dt`, Coriolis magnitude, or the ocean scheme could not fix an overwrite of `P`.

**What changed**

| Piece | New behavior |
|---|---|
| Air `P` | Prognostic: `∂P/∂t = −c² ρ ∇·u` plus the ocean-style forward–backward damper |
| Coupling | Nudges `P` toward thermal `P(T)` on a **~1.5 day** timescale — Hadley/Walker stay thermally driven, eddies are not wiped each step |
| Coriolis | Traditional `f = 2Ω sinφ` (β effect) |
| Drag | `4e-6 s⁻¹` (~3 day e-fold) |
| Thermal wind | Hydrostatic `~180 Pa/K` (a real 5 km column), so the jet is ~10 m/s surface / ~21 m/s aloft |
