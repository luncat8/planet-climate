### 2. Simulation Problems & Physical Critique
Despite the good signs, there are three main issues preventing this from looking like "real" weather:

**A. Excessive Numerical Diffusion ("Mushy" Storms)**
*   **Observation:** Look at the mid-latitude clouds and wind patterns. They look like smeared bands rather than distinct, rotating cyclones (low-pressure systems). Real weather has sharp fronts and swirling storms; yours looks smooth and blurry.
*   **Cause:** Your advection scheme in `OCEAN_FS`/`AIR_FS` uses simple first-order upwinding (`mix(tr0, trj, w)`). This is very stable but artificially smears out gradients over time. It kills the formation of sharp storm fronts.

**B. Lack of Asymmetry (Too "Generic")**
*   **Observation:** The weather patterns look somewhat symmetric around the globe. Real Earth has distinct monsoons, blocking highs, and specific storm tracks caused by continents and mountains.
*   **Cause:** Your land mask is procedural noise. While it breaks perfect symmetry, it lacks the thermal inertia contrast of real oceans vs. continents (land heats/cools faster than water) and lacks topography (mountains block wind).

**C. Weak Seasonal Response**
*   **Observation:** The ITCZ band looks fairly centered on the equator in both images. On Earth, this band migrates significantly north/south with the seasons.
*   **Cause:** Your seasonal forcing (`uSeasonDecl`) is present, but the thermal inertia of your surface layers might be too high to allow the temperature maximum (and thus the rising air) to migrate quickly enough.

*   **The Problem:** You are using explicit Euler integration with a fixed `dt = 300s`.
*   **The Risk:** If you increase resolution, your cell size shrinks by half. However, wind speed stays the same. This violates the **CFL condition**: fluid cannot travel further than one cell width per time step.
