## 2. Why L7 is 10 fps (16× for 4× cells)

L7 = 163,842 cells (256×641) vs L6 40,962 (256×161). The frame isn't draw-bound at L7 — the 16× decomposes:

- **×4 — cells.** 45 grid-res passes/frame each do 4× the pixels; globe+cloud vertex stage 4×.
- **×2–4 — the L2 cliff (the extra factor, main suspect).** Each dynamics pass touches ~9 textures → per-pass working set **L6 ≈ 5.9 MB (fits L2) → L7 ≈ 23.6 MB (DRAM-bound)**. With ~1,100 dependent texture fetches per cell per frame, per-fragment cost inflates several-fold. 4 × ~3–4 ≈ your 16×. (BENCH.md's "L7 ≈ 4× slower" reference was measured in a regime where this cliff didn't bite — worth re-confirming with the Phase-0 ms/cell table.)
- **45 unbatches draws** — per-pass bind/uniform/draw latency, never amortized (substeps × 8 pay it 8×).
- **Globe/cloud VS 1-ring pre-blend**: 16–21 dependent fetches *per vertex* × 2 draws × 164 k verts at L7.
- **If CO₂ is on**: 3 single-fragment reductions each serially looping all 163,842 cells + 3 `readPixels(1×1)` = 3 full pipeline stalls *every frame*.
- (If implicit: BENCH shows the Jacobi penalty grows ×3→×6 per level, widening it further.)

The plan's Phase 0 includes a toggle-bisect matrix (substeps 1/8, flow, CO₂, scheme, view) to confirm each term on your GPU in an hour.
